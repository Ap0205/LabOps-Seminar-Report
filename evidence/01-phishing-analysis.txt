TASK 1: IDENTIFYING AND PREVENTING PHISHING ATTACKS

The demonstration login page was opened locally in Safari.

Five warning signs discussed:

1. The address uses a local file:// path rather than an organisation's verified domain.
2. The page does not use an HTTPS website connection.
3. The heading is generic and does not identify a legitimate service.
4. There is no recognisable organisational branding.
5. There is no visible privacy notice, contact information or explanation for requesting credentials.

Safety observation:
This is a local educational demonstration. The form does not collect, transmit or store passwords.

Protection:
Check the website address carefully, use trusted bookmarks, avoid unexpected login links, and use multifactor authentication.

Reflection:

How can users recognise phishing pages?
Users should inspect the URL, check the domain carefully, look for HTTPS, and be cautious of unexpected login requests.

How can users protect themselves?
Use multifactor authentication, password managers, trusted bookmarks, updated browsers, and avoid clicking suspicious links.

Why do modern websites make phishing more difficult?
Modern websites use HTTPS, multifactor authentication, passkeys, domain protections, and suspicious-login detection. These measures reduce risk but do not eliminate phishing.
